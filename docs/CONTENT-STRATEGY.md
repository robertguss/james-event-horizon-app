# Event Horizon content strategy

**Status:** Product and content source of truth, August 2026

**Audience:** Future product, content, design, and engineering sessions

**Related:** [Product brief](PRODUCT-BRIEF.md), [design system](../DESIGN.md),
[Week 1 Day 1 pack](missions/week-01-day-01.md)

This document records the decisions made with Robert for the reading
comprehension app he is building for his son, James. Preserve these decisions
unless Robert explicitly changes them.

## 1. James and the learning goal

- James is eight and entering fourth grade. He is homeschooled using Classical
  Conversations and Memoria Press, but Event Horizon does not need to follow a
  formal standards framework.
- He is an excellent decoder and fluent reader. His difficulty is comprehension:
  he tends to miss the main idea and the connections among details, then
  struggles to explain what he read in his own words.
- He loves space, black holes, his telescope, Zelda games, LEGO, creativity, and
  the Bible. He has observed the Moon, stars, and planets through his telescope.
- He enjoys Zelda's complete appeal: exploration, puzzles, secrets, equipment
  collection and upgrades, combat, and story. Event Horizon should translate
  that appeal into original, nonviolent space exploration rather than copy
  Nintendo characters, places, or lore.
- The primary goal is that James asks to play. The learning sequence should be
  rigorous underneath, but the kid-facing experience should feel like a game,
  not schoolwork.

### Core learning outcome

James should learn to explain:

1. what a passage is mainly about;
2. which facts support that main idea;
3. how those facts connect;
4. what caused an event and what resulted from it; and
5. the passage in his own words.

Main idea, cause and effect, sequencing, evidence, and inference are the
priority skills. Vocabulary matters when it supports comprehension, but should
not displace the central goal.

## 2. Product and instructional approach

### Game first, instruction underneath

James sees missions, discoveries, telescope scans, hidden paths, observatory
repairs, and modular equipment. Parents may see explicit skill labels and
progress. Avoid kid-facing labels such as "main-idea worksheet" or "lesson
objective."

The adventure uses original IP. A good Zelda-inspired pattern is exploration,
environmental puzzles, secrets, tools that open new paths, and a larger mystery.
Do not use franchise characters, iconography, locations, terminology, or
lookalike art.

LEGO-inspired creativity should appear as modular building rather than branded
LEGO content. Understanding transmissions can unlock telescope, rover, ship, and
observatory pieces that James combines creatively instead of receiving only
abstract badges.

### Initial cadence

- One mission each weekday, Monday through Friday.
- Initial target: 12–15 minutes per mission.
- Passage length grows from roughly 160 words toward 250 words during the first
  month. Later seasons may grow longer as comprehension stamina improves.
- Optional bonus missions are welcome when James wants to continue, but the
  required weekday workload stays small.
- Parent recordings should be reviewed a few times per week, not after every
  play session.

### Target mission loop

1. **Mission brief** — give James a reason to read.
2. **Transmission** — authored, source-reviewed passage.
3. **Telescope scans** — evidence, main idea, vocabulary when useful, and
   inference/connection questions.
4. **Connection map** — arrange visual pieces into main idea, supporting facts,
   and cause/effect or sequence.
5. **Captain's Log** — record a 30–60 second explanation while looking at the
   completed map.
6. **Discovery reward** — unlock a creative module or observatory repair.

The narration scaffold is:

> This transmission is mainly about ____. One important fact is ____. That
> connects to the main idea because ____.

Do not reward speed. Reward evidence, revision, and a complete explanation.

### Parent review

Recordings enter a small Captain's Log inbox. Completing a mission must not wait
for parent review. The useful parent response is one encouragement plus one
focused follow-up, for example:

> You clearly explained what the diagonal did. Why could the square lean before
> the diagonal was added?

Parent review checks:

- main idea stated;
- at least two important facts included;
- connection explained;
- own words used rather than passage recitation.

## 3. Content mix

Use both factual and fictional reading, weighted strongly toward factual space
science. Non-space subjects are important so comprehension transfers beyond a
single interest.

The settled five-mission weekly rhythm is:

- three factual space-science missions;
- one Bible mission; and
- one building, engineering, or original adventure mission.

Science passages should be original writing grounded in authoritative sources
such as NASA, ESA, the Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration, and reputable
engineering education sources. Keep source URLs in mission documentation.

## 4. Bible content

- The family normally uses the ESV and Vos's _The Child's Story Bible_.
- Bible missions do not need to track the family's current reading schedule.
- Use a mixture of faithful, age-appropriate original retellings and short exact
  ESV excerpts.
- Treat Vos as family context, not source text. Do not copy its copyrighted
  wording.
- Bible missions use a distinct, calm, reverent **Scripture Archive**
  presentation. Do not place biblical events inside the fictional space
  universe, turn them into boss battles, or add fictional events to Scripture.
- Scripture Archive work may award normal XP, but celebration should stay quiet
  and dignified.
- Questions should first distinguish what the text says from application or
  doctrinal interpretation. Robert and his wife should review theological
  wording before publication.

### ESV usage

Crossway's standard digital-use guidance currently permits up to 500 verses
without a formal license when its other limits and attribution requirements are
met. The ESV may not make up 25% or more of the work, quotations may not exceed
half of a biblical book, and required notices must be included. Crossway also
offers a free noncommercial ESV API. Recheck the current terms before public
distribution:

- <https://www.crossway.org/permissions>
- <https://api.esv.org/>

## 6. Week 1 content direction

### Day 1 — The Telescope's Light Collector

Teach that telescopes use a main lens or mirror to gather and focus light, and
that a larger aperture gathers more light to reveal fainter objects. Explicitly
counter the misconception that a telescope pulls an object closer. End with a
visual chain:

`faint object → lens/mirror gathers light → light reaches focus → image reaches eye/camera`

The complete authored pack is in
[week-01-day-01.md](missions/week-01-day-01.md).

### Day 2 — The Moon's Changing Face

Teach that the Moon reflects sunlight, half is always illuminated, and the
visible portion changes as the Moon orbits Earth. Explicitly counter the common
misconception that Earth's shadow normally causes Moon phases. End by arranging
Sun, Earth, Moon, and four phase cards.

Source: <https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-phases/>

### Day 3 — Why Stars Twinkle

Teach that moving pockets of air in Earth's atmosphere bend the point-like light
from distant stars, producing apparent changes in brightness or position.
Planets usually twinkle less because they appear as tiny disks whose distorted
light paths average out. End with
`star → moving atmosphere → changing light path → apparent twinkle`.

Source: <https://skyandtelescope.org/astronomy-resources/why-do-stars-twinkle/>

### Day 4 — Scripture Archive: God Calls Abram

Use a faithful original retelling and selected exact text from Genesis 12:1–9
ESV. Organize the narration around
`God commands → God promises → Abram responds`. A useful ESV vocabulary item is
_kindred_. Keep the presentation reverent and do not depict God as a draggable
or cartoon character.

Source: <https://www.esv.org/Genesis+12:1%E2%80%939/>

### Day 5 — Build Lab: Stop the Wobble

Teach the precise engineering idea rather than the oversimplified claim that a
triangle is always the "strongest shape." A square frame with pivoting joints
can deform into a parallelogram; a diagonal brace creates two rigid triangles
that resist that shape change. End by adding a diagonal to a wobbling frame.

Source:
<https://undergroundmathematics.org/thinking-about-geometry/triangles-are-the-strongest-shape>

## 7. Deferred ideas

### AI voice tutor

Deferred to a later phase. The eventual tutor should be a tightly bounded
Mission Control interaction that asks James for the main idea, evidence, and
connections without giving answers. It must not become open-ended chat.

Before any child-facing AI voice implementation:

- reconfirm provider terms for users under 13;
- require parental consent and initially parent supervision;
- minimize data and avoid retaining raw audio unnecessarily;
- enforce passage-only topic boundaries and static fallbacks; and
- review COPPA and other applicable child-privacy requirements.

OpenAI Realtime is technically suitable for browser speech over WebRTC, but
OpenAI's current child-use guidance makes unsupervised use inappropriate to
assume without API-specific confirmation.

### Typing

James does not type yet and currently uses the iPad microphone for search.
Typing instruction is a promising later Academy or "flight computer" feature,
but it must not block the initial comprehension loop.

## 8. Implementation status

As of August 2026, the app has:

- all five Week 1 missions authored and registered in the shared fixture and
  Convex mission catalog;
- eight- or nine-sentence passages, five graded questions, four-step hint
  ladders, and source notes for each Week 1 mission;
- a touch-first Connection Map that James completes by arranging authored pieces
  in order;
- an optional 30–60 second Captain's Log recorded with the browser microphone,
  with no AI analysis or transcription;
- a parent Captain's Log inbox with mission-specific listening guidance,
  playback, and permanent deletion;
- Convex storage and ownership checks for hosted recordings; and
- in-memory object-URL storage for fixture-mode recordings, which lasts only for
  the current app process and is intended for local demonstration rather than
  durable family use.

The previous _Dust Storm on Mars_ mission remains available after Week 1, and
the Saturn, comet, and black-hole stubs remain in the catalog. Weeks 2–4 are
planned but not yet fully authored or playable. The AI voice tutor and typing
instruction remain deferred.

The current Connection Map uses large tappable text pieces rather than custom
illustrations or free dragging. This intentionally supports iPad use without
requiring typing or precise drag gestures; richer artwork can be added without
changing the comprehension sequence.
