## Objective

Explain how a telescope gathers and focuses light to reveal faint objects.

## Mission brief

The Great Observatory has detected a faint object far beyond the solar system,
but its damaged telescope sees only a blurry smudge. Restore the light collector
and determine which telescope design can reveal the hidden object.

## Transmission

| id   | text                                                                                                                              |
| ---- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `s1` | Astronomers sometimes aim a telescope at a galaxy so faint that it looks like a tiny smudge, even under a clear night sky.        |
| `s2` | A telescope does not pull the galaxy closer; instead, it helps the observer use more of the light that has traveled across space. |
| `s3` | Its main lens or mirror gathers light over an area much larger than the opening of a human eye.                                   |
| `s4` | The curved lens or mirror then directs those light rays toward one place, called the focus, where an image forms.                 |
| `s5` | A larger main lens or mirror collects more light than a smaller one, so it can reveal fainter objects and details.                |
| `s6` | A refracting telescope bends light through lenses, while a reflecting telescope bounces light from mirrors.                       |
| `s7` | An eyepiece enlarges the focused image for an observer, and a camera can record the light for scientists to study.                |
| `s8` | To find the observatory's faint target, the crew needs a telescope that gathers plenty of light and focuses it accurately.        |

## Questions

### Q1 — Locate evidence

**Prompt:** Which sentence explains how a telescope forms an image from the
light it gathers?

**Correct evidence:** `s4` only.

**Distractor traps:** `s3`, `s5`, `s7`.

Hints:

1. Look for what happens to the light after it is gathered.
2. Find the sentence describing where the curved lens or mirror sends the light.
3. Decide between gathering light, directing light to one place, and recording
   the finished image.
4. Glow `s4`, but still require the evidence tap.

### Q2 — Main idea

**Prompt:** What is this transmission mostly about?

- A. Telescopes pull distant galaxies closer to Earth.
- B. Telescopes gather and focus light so faint, distant objects can be
  observed.
- C. Cameras create the light that astronomers see.

**Correct:** B.

Hints:

1. Ask what the lens or mirror does in several parts of the transmission.
2. The best main idea should include both gathering and focusing.
3. Cross out choices claiming that a telescope moves an object or creates light.
4. Soft-highlight the gathering-and-focusing sentences without selecting an
   answer.

### Q3 — Vocabulary in context

**Prompt:** In this transmission, what does _focus_ mean?

- A. The place where gathered light comes together and forms an image.
- B. The handle used to carry a telescope.
- C. A period of clear weather without clouds.

**Correct:** A.

**Context:** `s4`.

Hints:

1. Reread what happens at the focus.
2. Is the passage describing a place, a handle, or the weather?
3. Look for the place where the directed light rays meet.
4. Glow `s4` while still requiring a choice.

### Q4 — Inference plus evidence

**Prompt:** The crew can install one of two equally well-made telescopes. One
has a 7-centimeter main lens, and the other has a 20-centimeter main lens. Which
should they use to detect the faint galaxy?

- A. The 7-centimeter lens because a smaller lens produces more light.
- B. The 20-centimeter lens because a larger lens gathers more light.
- C. Either one because only the eyepiece determines what can be seen.

**Correct:** B plus evidence `s5`.

Hints:

1. The target is faint, so think about how much light the telescope needs.
2. Find the sentence comparing larger and smaller lenses or mirrors.
3. Choose the telescope that gathers more light, then prove it from the passage.
4. Glow `s5` without automatically selecting it.

### Q5 — Exit ticket

**Prompt:** What should the crew remember while rebuilding the observatory
telescope?

- A. Its main lens or mirror must gather and focus light, and a larger one can
  reveal fainter objects.
- B. It must pull distant objects closer to the observatory.
- C. Its camera must create new light before an image can form.

**Correct:** A.

**Hint anchor:** `s8`.

## Connection map

Arrange:

`faint galaxy sends light → lens or mirror gathers light → light reaches the focus → image reaches eye or camera`

Then complete:

`larger main lens or mirror → more light gathered → fainter objects revealed`

Distractors:

- The telescope pulls the galaxy closer.
- The camera creates starlight.
- The telescope sends a flashlight beam to the galaxy.

## Captain's Log

Prompt:

> Explain how a telescope helps astronomers see a faint galaxy. Include what the
> main lens or mirror does, what happens at the focus, and why the size of the
> lens or mirror matters.

Optional speaking supports:

- This transmission is mainly about…
- First, the lens or mirror…
- Next, the gathered light…
- A larger lens or mirror helps because…

Parent-only answer model:

> The transmission is mainly about how telescopes gather and focus light. The
> main lens or mirror collects more light than an eye can. Its curved surface
> sends the light to the focus, where an image forms. A larger lens or mirror
> gathers more light, so astronomers can see fainter objects.

## Reward and real-world extension

Resolve the faint smudge into a galaxy and unlock an **Aperture Ring**. Its
description reinforces: "A wider opening gathers more light from faint objects."

With an adult, compare a familiar patch of night sky with unaided eyes and
through a telescope, then explain what additional details become visible. Never
point a telescope at the Sun without a properly fitted, certified solar filter
and adult supervision.
